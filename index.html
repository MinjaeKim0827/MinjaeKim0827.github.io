About Me

I am a physics undergraduate at KAIST (B.S. expected February 2027, minor in Electrical Engineering). I am interested in studying galactic ecosystems, especially from the perspective of observational cosmology. Since joining Prof. Junhan Kim's OBSCAL group at KAIST in Fall 2024, I have pursued two complementary questions about how galaxies and their black holes grow:

  • The hot circumgalactic medium. Using the IllustrisTNG cosmological simulations, I model the intrinsic soft X-ray emission of hot gas around galaxies to understand how feedback reshapes it as galaxies stop forming stars.
  • The earliest luminous quasars. On the SPHEREx team at KASI, I am building a spectral-fitting pipeline for SPHEREx's low-resolution near-infrared spectra to measure the rest-frame optical properties of luminous quasars at z > 5.

I am applying to Ph.D. programs in astronomy & astrophysics for Fall 2027. My long-term goal is to become an observational astronomer who works across observations, simulations, and instrumentation.

Research

Intrinsic Soft X-ray Emission from the Hot Circumgalactic Medium in IllustrisTNG Aug 2024 – Present
OBSCAL Group, KAIST · Advisor: Prof. Junhan Kim
  • Modeling the intrinsic soft X-ray emission of hot circumgalactic gas in the IllustrisTNG cosmological simulations, with emissivities computed using pyatomdb/APEC.
  • Fitting X-ray scaling relations with MCMC (emcee), including intrinsic scatter, and testing the LX–SFR relation across galaxy populations with partial-correlation analysis.
Key finding: quiescent galaxies show no true deficit in hot-gas content or thermal energy relative to star-forming galaxies. Their hot gas is redistributed to larger radii rather than expelled, which lowers the X-ray luminosity by about an order of magnitude.
  • Began through the KAIST Undergraduate Research Program (URP, Spring 2025); received the URP Excellent Award and was selected for follow-up research support (Dec 2025 – Jun 2026).
  • First-author paper submitted to ApJ (Oct 2026); preprint available on arXiv:2610.16200.
Spectral Analysis of High-Redshift (z > 5) Quasars with SPHEREx Jan 2026 – Present
SPHEREx Team, Korea Astronomy and Space Science Institute (KASI) · Supervisors: Dr. Bomee Lee & Dr. Woong-Seob Jeong
  • Started through the KASI–UST Winter Internship (Jan–Feb 2026); continuing as a part-time research assistant, with contract reappointment across multiple terms.
  • Developing a Python pipeline for SPHEREx's low-resolution (R ≈ 35–130) spectra: sample compilation and source matching, instrument characterization, forward-modeled SED fitting with dust-reddening correction, and SED analysis.
  • Identifying spectral features and deriving the physical properties of the quasars with statistical methods.
  • First-author manuscript in progress with Dr. Bomee Lee.
Kinematical Analysis of M32: Serendipitous versus Targeted Stars Summer – Winter 2025
UC Santa Cruz, International Summer Research Program (ISRP) · Advisor: Prof. Puragra (Raja) GuhaThakurta
  • Compared the kinematics of serendipitously observed and targeted stars around M32 and assessed the effect of spectral blending.
  • Led the project while mentoring three high school interns in the Science Internship Program (SIP); the results were presented at the 247th AAS Meeting (Jan 2026) by my mentee, with me as a co-author.
